20 DIY Halloween Outdoor Decorations You Can Make This Weekend

By Trend Choice Team
09/13/2026

Not every great Halloween porch comes from a box. DIY Halloween outdoor decorations tend to look more personal (and cost a fraction of store-bought versions), and most of the ideas below use supplies you can find at a craft store, hardware store, or already have at home.

Below are 20 DIY Halloween outdoor decoration ideas, organized by how much time and skill each one takes, so you can pick projects that fit an afternoon or a full weekend.

 

What You'll Need Before You Start

Most projects below draw from a small shared supply list: white or cream sheets/fabric (for ghosts), mason jars or tin cans (for lanterns), craft paint and a hot glue gun, plain wood boards or pallets (for signs), and battery-powered tea lights so nothing needs an outlet. Buying these basics once means you can knock out several projects from this list without a separate trip for each one.

Quick Projects (Under 30 Minutes)

1. Painted Mason Jar Lanterns

Paint the outside of a mason jar with black or orange glass craft paint, scrape off a simple jack-o'-lantern face while the paint is still wet, and drop in a battery tea light once it dries.

2. Toilet Paper Roll Bats

Flatten and cut toilet paper rolls into bat silhouettes, paint them black, and mount them in a scattered flying formation on a porch wall or door — a classic kid-friendly project that costs almost nothing.

3. Painted Rock Pumpkins

Smooth garden rocks painted orange with a black stem and face make surprisingly durable, weatherproof mini pumpkins to line a walkway or windowsill.

4. Spray-Painted Branch "Spider Legs"

Black spray-painted branches, arranged fan-style behind a porch light or in a planter, read as oversized spider legs with almost no assembly required.

5. Chalkboard Welcome Sign

A small chalkboard (or a board painted with chalkboard paint) propped by the door with a hand-lettered "Trick or Treat" message is a five-minute project you can update every year.

Weekend Afternoon Projects

6. Cheesecloth Ghosts

Stretch cheesecloth or thin white fabric over a wire frame or balloon, let it dry stiffened with fabric starch, and hang the finished ghost shape from a tree branch or porch eave.

7. Wood Pallet "Boo" Sign

Disassemble part of a wood pallet, sand the boards, and paint or stencil "BOO" across them for a rustic sign that works with almost any decor style.

8. DIY Spider Web from Yarn

Black or white yarn strung between porch posts in a web pattern, secured with small nails or hooks, covers a large area for very little material cost.

9. Tin Can Luminaries

Hammer a simple star or moon pattern of holes into a clean tin can, then set a tea light inside — grouped in odd numbers along steps, they throw dramatic shadow patterns after dark.

10. Fabric Ghost Garland

Small fabric ghost shapes cut from an old sheet and strung on twine make an easy garland for a railing or window, and store flat for reuse next year.

11. Glowing Pathway Stones

Painted rocks or stepping stones coated with a layer of glow-in-the-dark paint along a walkway light up faintly after dark without needing any batteries or wiring.

12. Milk Jug Ghost Lights

Draw simple ghost faces on empty white milk jugs, drop a battery tea light or string light inside each, and line them along a walkway for a soft glowing effect.

13. Wine Bottle Witches

Empty wine or glass bottles painted black with a witch hat topper (foam or paper cone) make a quirky, easy-to-store porch accent.

14. Burlap & Twine Wreath

A plain grapevine or foam wreath base wrapped in burlap ribbon, with a few silk fall leaves or a small wood pumpkin glued on, costs a fraction of a store-bought seasonal wreath.

15. Painted Window Silhouettes

Black cardstock cut into bat, cat, or witch silhouettes and taped inside a front window creates a dramatic effect visible from the street, especially once the porch light is on behind it.

Bigger Weekend Projects

16. Wood Skeleton Cutout

A skeleton shape cut from plywood with a jigsaw, painted white with black joint details, and staked into the lawn is a sturdier, more weatherproof alternative to a plastic skeleton prop.

17. Scarecrow from Old Clothes

Stuffed old clothes over a wood stake frame, topped with a stuffed pillowcase head and a straw hat, makes a full-size scarecrow for the cost of supplies you likely already have.

18. PVC Pipe Tombstones

Foam board or thin plywood tombstone shapes, painted gray with a stenciled epitaph, propped in the yard with a hidden stake, build a graveyard scene for very little per piece.

19. Hay Bale & Pumpkin Display

A stacked hay bale base topped with a mix of real and painted pumpkins creates an instant focal point that also doubles as extra porch seating.

20. String Light Spiderweb

String lights woven in a web pattern across a porch ceiling or between two posts combine two DIY projects (lighting and web) into one and look far more elaborate than the actual effort required.

Tips for DIY Halloween Decorating

Start with the quick projects first — a few painted mason jars and rock pumpkins build momentum before tackling something like a wood skeleton cutout. Use battery-powered lights wherever possible so placement isn't limited by outlet access, and store finished pieces flat or stacked in labeled bins so next year's setup takes an afternoon instead of a full weekend again. If a project uses real branches, hay, or other organic material, expect it to only last one season — that's normal, not a sign you did something wrong.

FAQ: DIY Halloween Outdoor Decorations

What are the easiest DIY Halloween outdoor decorations to start with?

Painted mason jar lanterns, painted rock pumpkins, and a chalkboard sign are the fastest wins — each takes under 30 minutes and uses supplies most craft stores carry cheaply in bulk during fall.

Will DIY Halloween decorations hold up outside for a month?

Painted and sealed pieces (rocks, wood, tin) hold up well. Fabric and cheesecloth projects do better under a covered porch than fully exposed to rain — bring them in during storms if possible.

Can I make DIY Halloween decorations without power tools?

Yes — most of the quick and weekend-afternoon projects (jars, rocks, fabric, cardstock, yarn) need nothing beyond scissors, paint, and a glue gun. Only the bigger builds like the wood skeleton cutout benefit from a jigsaw.

 

Final Thoughts

You don't need every project on this list — pick two or three quick wins and one bigger weekend build, and you'll already have a porch that looks intentional rather than thrown together. Save what works well and build on it each year.
